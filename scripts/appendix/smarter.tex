\paragraph{Task.} Classification of single English social-media posts: HateXplain
(\textsc{Normal}/\textsc{Offensive}/\textsc{Hate}), Latent Hate (\textsc{Not}/\textsc{Explicit}/\textsc{Implicit
Hate}) and Implicit Hate, which assigns implicit-hate tweets to fine-grained categories. Implicit Hate has
<<s:smarter/implicit_hate:per_class_f1|len>> classes, not three as our preregistration row states. The replaced
predictors are the paper's self-augmented (SFT+DPO) Llama-3.1-8B and COT-T5-XL classifiers. \jev{} only chooses
the label.

\paragraph{Data and freeze.} The authors' code is public
(\url{https://github.com/hnghiem-nlp/hate_dpo_public} at <<m:smarter:paper.code.commit|commit>>), but their test files
were never released. Sources: HateXplain (\url{https://github.com/hate-alert/HateXplain} at
<<m:smarter:sources.hatexplain.commit|commit>>, MIT) is frozen with text (SHA-256
<<m:smarter:frozen.hatexplain.sha256|sha>>). The Latent Hatred corpus zip (\url{https://github.com/SALT-NLP/implicit-hate},
CC BY 4.0) is not redistributed: its frozen files hold tweet IDs, row indices, gold labels and per-row SHA-256s, and
the text is rebuilt from the zip and verified.

\paragraph{Test set and screen.} The test sets are reconstructions made by \texttt{scripts/prepare\_smarter.py},
using the authors' own \texttt{split\_train\_test} and \texttt{k\_shot\_sample} code with the code's default
seeds and the Table~4 split ratios and per-class sizes. Scored $N=$ <<s:smarter/hatexplain:n|int>>, <<s:smarter/latent_hate:n|int>> and
<<s:smarter/implicit_hate:n|int>>. Latent Hate's \textsc{Explicit Hate} class contributes all
<<s:smarter/latent_hate:n_gold.Explicit Hate|int>> of its test-split rows, because the release has too few for the
paper's per-class sample. The language screen (langid, non-ASCII ratio and function words, every flag
checked) removed <<s:smarter/hatexplain:n_excluded_non_english|int>> non-English HateXplain posts.

\paragraph{Prompt.} One \textsc{<<q:smarter_hatexplain:_primitive|upper>>} per post (state = \{post\}). The
instruction is the paper's classification prompt without explanation (Fig.~6), and the criteria quote its Table~10
label definitions verbatim. Prediction: argmax, ties broken in Table~4 order.

\paragraph{Deviations and status.} Reconstructed test set: the sources, label spaces, ratios, seeds and sample
sizes follow the authors' code, but row identity with the unreleased files cannot be verified. \jev{} is well below
the fine-tuned models. It falls within the range of the paper's zero-shot LLMs on HateXplain and Latent Hate, and
near the weakest of them on Implicit Hate. Its predictions are skewed: it predicts \textsc{Hate}
<<s:smarter/hatexplain:n_pred.Hate|int>> times against <<s:smarter/hatexplain:n_gold.Hate|int>> gold, and
\textsc{Stereotypes and Misinformation} <<s:smarter/implicit_hate:n_pred.Stereotypes and Misinformation|int>> times
against <<s:smarter/implicit_hate:n_gold.Stereotypes and Misinformation|int>>. HateXplain's no-majority posts and
Implicit Hate's out-of-taxonomy \texttt{other} posts were removed before splitting.
