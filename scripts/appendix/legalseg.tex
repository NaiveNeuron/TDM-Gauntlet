\paragraph{Task.} Rhetorical role labelling of English Indian court judgments: every sentence gets one of seven roles
(Facts, Issue, Arguments of Petitioner/Respondent, Reasoning, Decision, None). Replaced predictors: the paper's
hierarchical and context-aware sequence classifiers.

\paragraph{Data and freeze.} The canonical \texttt{LegalSeg\_CSV} test file is gated and was not used. The text comes
from the authors' ungated RhetoricLLaMA prediction release (\texttt{L-NLProc/}\allowbreak\texttt{LegalSeg\_RhetoricLLaMA\_}\allowbreak\texttt{Predictions},
SHA-256 <<m:legalseg:source.raw_file_sha256|sha>>), which is the full test split: document, sentence and per-role
counts equal paper Table~2, and its gold equals that of the authors' ToInLegalBERT predictions at every position.
Apache-2.0. \texttt{scripts/prepare\_legalseg.py} stores only gold codes and hashes and rebuilds the items.

\paragraph{Test set and English screen.} $N=$ <<s:legalseg:n|int>> sentences in <<s:legalseg:n_docs|int>> judgments:
the paper's test split minus <<s:legalseg:n_excluded_non_english|int>> sentences of romanised Hindi quoted speech,
found by langid and a romanised-Hindi keyword scan (all flags read).

\paragraph{Prompt.} One \textsc{choice} per sentence, a block of consecutive sentences per request. State =
\{document description, excerpt note, role definitions (verbatim, paper \S4.5), numbered sentences\}. The instruction
adapts the paper's instruction set~1 (Table~5) and is zero-shot. A judgment that fits the state budget is shown whole in
every request. Longer ones (<<s:legalseg:n_truncated|int>>) are shown as windows around each block. Sentences are
classified independently (no CRF decoding). Argmax. Ties to None first.

\paragraph{Metric.} Macro-F1 over the seven roles, pooled over sentences (Table~3). Accuracy and MCC secondary.

\paragraph{Deviations and status.} Complete. The paper's headline Hier-BiLSTM-CRF macro-F1 cannot be reproduced from
the authors' released predictions (the sentence-level file appears mis-paired, and the document-level file, aligned for
most documents, scores far lower, as noted under the table), whereas
ToInLegalBERT's row reproduces exactly. We treat ToInLegalBERT as the strongest reliable baseline. \jev{}'s main error
is None: it predicts the role far less often than the gold (counts above), mostly choosing Reasoning or Facts instead.
